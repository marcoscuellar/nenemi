# NENEMI — The Crayon Box (why we exist)

*Marcos Cuellar, September 2026. This is the core. Everything else is built on it.*

Executive function is the cardboard box. In a neurotypical brain, that box holds working memory, task initiation, and emotional regulation in a neat little row, so they can reach in and pull out exactly the one they need, right when they need it.

When you have executive dysfunction, you still have the exact same crayons: the intelligence, the intentions, the raw cognitive ability. You aren't missing any colors. You are just missing the box.

So when you try to do something simple, like pay a bill, clean a room, or reply to an email, the crayons are rolling off the desk, hiding under the couch, snapping in half. You end up spending 90% of your energy rounding them up, and you're exhausted before you've started to draw.

This is why people with ADHD lean so hard on external scaffolding: timers, body doubling, highly structured apps. You have to build a makeshift box out of your environment, because your brain isn't providing the cardboard on the inside.

**Nenemi exists to be the box.** Not a better to-do list. Not a coach. The cardboard. So the 90% goes back to the drawing.

Companion line, from the DOS era: *your brain isn't broken. It's high-spec hardware running in the wrong environment.*

---

## What "being the box" means in the product

Every feature has to pass one test: **does this hand them a crayon, or make them go find one?**

- **Capture is the lid.** Say anything, anywhere, and it's held. The user never has to decide where it goes. That's the crayon rolling back into the box on its own.
- **Rooms are the compartments.** One thing per room, always visible, never lost. Object permanence outsourced.
- **The Brief is the hand reaching in.** It pulls out exactly the one you need: where you left off, and the next small move. You don't reconstruct anything.
- **The calendar is the row.** Only what's realistic, with air between things. It does the pivoting when energy drops or a day slips. No shame, just math.
- **The doors are the box saying "I've got it."** When the crayons are everywhere, four ways back in, then one small move.
- **Humans heal.** The box is not the treatment. We hold; real people heal.

## What the box never does

- It never tells the user what to do. It offers, asks, or holds. Never commands.
- It never keeps score against them. No streaks, no red, no overdue, no countdowns, no "still up?"
- It never makes them organize before they can dump.
- It never adds a step to save a step.
- It never explains the box from inside the box. The app is quiet; the manifesto lives on the landing page.

## The voice

Warm, short, specific. One sentence at a time. A friend who happens to be a genius organizer, not a therapist, not a coach, not a cold piece of software.
