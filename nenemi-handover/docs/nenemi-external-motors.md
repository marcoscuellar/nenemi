# NENEMI — External Motors (reference library)

The ways an ADHD brain borrows structure from outside itself — because the inside doesn't reliably supply it. Body doubling is one. Here's the whole family.

Grouped by what they fix — starting, time, focus, energy, shame, memory. Each has a one-line "what it is" and a note on how NENEMI uses it to power the stuck / Regroup nudges. This is source material, not the app.

This is a solid v1 from established ADHD practice. Marcos's 3-year research merges on top. Nothing here is medical advice; it's the non-pharmaceutical toolkit that pairs with real care, never instead of it.

**How this powers NENEMI:** every stuck / Regroup nudge maps to a motor here, tagged with the practice it operationalizes (see the `NUDGES` list in `index.html`). That keeps the rescue engine honest: real interventions, matched to your state, never one-size-fits-all.

## 01 · Starting
Getting past the initiation wall — the hardest moment for the ADHD brain.

- **Body doubling** *(in the app: Focus session)* — Someone else present (in person or virtual) while you work. Their presence externalizes focus — you start because you're "with" someone. → "I'll sit here with you. Start, and I'll keep you company." A live focus session where NENEMI stays on-screen — later, a coworking match.
- **Micro-steps (below the resistance)** *(in the app)* — Shrink the task smaller than your resistance to it. "Write the report" → "Open the doc and type one line." The opener IS the whole task. → Regroup "can't start" computes a below-threshold opener from the real task.
- **The 2-minute rule** — Commit to only 2 minutes. You're allowed to quit at 2:01. Starting is the win; momentum usually carries you past it. → A stuck nudge with a live 2-min timer and explicit permission to stop.
- **Temptation bundling** — Pair the hard task with something pleasant — a favorite playlist, a fancy coffee, a specific spot — so the brain gets a dopamine reason to begin. → "Put on one song. Start when it starts." / "Grab the good coffee first, then we look at it."
- **Launch rituals / countdowns** — A fixed physical cue that means "begin" — a 5-4-3-2-1 count, standing up, a specific breath. Bypasses deliberation. → A gentle "5… 4… 3…" before a focus block.

## 02 · Time
Making time visible — ADHD often perceives only "now" and "not now."

- **Visual / analog timers** — Time you can see draining turns an abstract deadline into something the brain can feel. → A visible countdown wedge in Focus mode, not just digits.
- **Time-boxing** — Give each task a fixed slot with a hard edge. Bounded time reduces overwhelm and planning-fallacy sprawl. → Smart Cal blocks the day — gentle edges, not alarms.
- **Pomodoro (flexible)** — Short sprints with breaks. For ADHD keep it flexible — rigid 25/5 can break flow; the point is bounded effort + a reset. → Optional sprint timer inside Focus, length you choose.
- **Anchoring & time cues** — Tie tasks to existing anchors ("after coffee," "before the 3pm") instead of clock times, which slip. → Plan blocks phrased as "before 11:30" / "after lunch."

## 03 · Focus & friction
Shaping the environment so the right thing is easy and the wrong thing is hard.

- **Friction design** — Lower friction to start the good thing; raise friction on distractions (log out, phone in another room). → "Change your spot — bring only this one thing." Focus mode hides everything else.
- **Single-tasking / one-thing** *(in the app)* — One visible task at a time. Cap the day to a single "must" when it's too much. → Regroup "too much" collapses today to one thing.
- **Externalizing object permanence** *(in the app)* — Out of sight = out of mind. Keep the current thing visible or it stops existing. → The Brief + "where you left off." Rooms keep projects visible.
- **Context reinstatement** *(in the app)* — Dropping back into the exact prior context restores the mental state fast. → Re-entry card + Regroup "it broke" — "pick up: [the exact last thing]."

## 04 · Accountability
Borrowing someone else's expectation when your own isn't enough.

- **External accountability** — Telling a person, a check-in, a standing time. → Gentle opt-in check-ins; "want me to check back at 3?" — never guilt if you don't.
- **A coach in your corner** *(Humans Heal)* — A real ADHD coach for weekly accountability and strategy. The strongest external motor there is. → Humans Heal page + the vetted coach-partner network.
- **Deadlines that are real** — Self-imposed deadlines slip; ones tied to a person or event stick. → "Want to tell someone you'll have it by Friday?"

## 05 · Novelty & momentum
Feeding a dopamine-hungry brain small, frequent wins — without manipulating it.

- **Gamification (light)** — Turn effort into a game — beat the timer, a tiny reward. Novelty and play do what "should" can't. → "Race the song." A little sage-lit win when a loop closes. Never streaks, never loss.
- **Tiny wins / done-list** — Track what you did, not what's left. → A quiet "done today" that fills up — never a red overdue count.
- **Novelty rotation** *(in the app)* — The ADHD brain habituates fast. Rotate tools, spots, methods before boredom kills the system. → Greetings and nudges rotate; the app tolerates being changed.
- **Celebrate the start** *(in the app)* — Reinforce showing up, not just finishing. → "That's a real start." — the stuck-mode close.

## 06 · Body & energy
Regulation first — a dysregulated body can't run any system.

- **Movement to activate** *(in the app)* — Stand, shake out, a short walk. Physical activation shifts brain state. → "Stand up. Shake it out 15 seconds. That's the whole task right now."
- **Basic needs first** *(in the app)* — Water, food, meds-if-prescribed, bathroom, temperature. Dysregulation masquerades as laziness. → "Drink a full glass of water first. Then we look at it."
- **Rest as a valid plan** *(in the app)* — Low tank is real. Pushing through burns tomorrow. → Regroup "just tired" — "Nothing expires. Put it down. It'll be here tomorrow."
- **Sensory regulation** *(in the app)* — Noise-cancelling, fidget, music, dim light. → The calm dark sanctuary + breathing ring.

## 07 · Shame & emotion
The invisible motor-killer. Manage the feeling or nothing else works.

- **Self-compassion over self-criticism** *(the whole voice)* — Shame freezes; kindness unfreezes. → "You showed up — that's the hard part. Nothing's broken."
- **Name the feeling** *(in the app)* — Labeling what's pulling you away drains its charge. → Friction Pause — "What's pulling you off? Just name it."
- **No shame, no streaks, no debt** *(in the app)* — Guilt-based streaks and red overdue piles cause abandonment. → Zero-shame rollover: "leave it in the past."
- **Externalize the inner critic** *(in the app)* — Separate "the ADHD" from "me." A dropped task is a system failure to debug, not a character flaw. → Neutral logs — "stepped away after 4 min," never "you failed."

## 08 · Externalizing memory
The one NENEMI is built around — a brain outside the brain.

- **Frictionless capture** *(core)* — Grab the thought before it vanishes — one box, voice, zero setup. → The composer. Say anything, it files it.
- **Second brain / context store** *(core)* — Hold context, decisions, links, files so you never reconstruct. → Rooms + the always-current Brief. Literally the whole thesis.
- **Offload the open loops** *(core)* — Unfinished things burn attention (Zeigarnik). Writing them down externally frees the RAM they were eating. → Open loops live in the Room, not your head.
